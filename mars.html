Scientific background

A Mars-orbiting nanosatellite must orient its instrument toward the surface for observations, its antenna toward a geosynchronous relay spacecraft for communications, and its solar panels toward the Sun for power generation. This project examines the attitude geometry and tracking requirements associated with these three operational modes.

Technical contributions

Derived circular-orbit spacecraft states, constructed Hill and mission-pointing reference frames, and calculated reference angular velocities using direction cosine matrices. Formulated attitude and angular-velocity tracking errors using Modified Rodrigues Parameters. Supporting MATLAB simulations illustrate pointing-error convergence and control-torque demand during transitions between Sun, nadir and relay-pointing modes. Extended the simulation to relate instrument-to-nadir pointing accuracy to usable observation windows.

Reaction-wheel limits
